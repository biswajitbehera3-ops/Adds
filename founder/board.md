# Board, round 3 (7 Nov): ₹10,000 setup + ₹699 a month, 5+ chair salons, Bhubaneswar first

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score 4.7 / 10** (Offers 5 · Monopoly 4 · Product 5). Round 2 (22 Oct): 3 fund if, 4.3 (`board/round2/`). Round 1: 2 fund if, 1 pass, 3.7.
The lenses apply published frameworks; they are not the authors' opinions. Memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`; brief: `board/brief.md`.

## What got better since round 2
- The per-salon money works: one-tap WhatsApp keeps cost near ₹167 a month, so each salon leaves ~₹532 (76%). Round 2's loss at ₹500 with automatic WhatsApp is gone (Offers, Monopoly).
- Three round-2 conditions are done: competitors checked, one city picked (Bhubaneswar, 86 salons pass the filters), and a guarantee that measures real use (Monopoly, Offers, Product).
- In-person setup with a named person answers the most common public complaint about rivals ("support disappears after the sale") (all three).

## Risks more than one member raised
1. **Nothing is proven, and the core is unbuilt** (all three). Live sync is missing, so the live view across phones, online backup and the iPhone app don't exist yet. No real owner has used the app and no salon has paid.
2. **Growth depends on one person** (all three). Every salon needs the founder for the sale, the setup and support; one person supports about 60 salons. "Who sets up salon 61?" was asked by two members.
3. **The edge is service and price, which are easy to copy** (Offers, Monopoly). The live floor is not unique (at least two rivals show live chairs). ₹699 is the cheapest in the market and locked for 2 years, so the business competes on being cheapest.
4. **Staff must enter every customer** (Product; also round 2). If stylists skip entries at rush hour, the owner sees wrong numbers.

## The founder's questions: the board's answers
**Future (3–5 years).** All three see the same best case, said differently: the front-desk and customer-return tool for mid-size, top-rated salons in Bhubaneswar first, then copied city by city. Product says stay narrow ("the tool that brings customers back, done brilliantly"); Monopoly says build something that gets stronger with each salon (customer history, wallets, owners recommending owners); Offers says it needs proof, then people other than the founder doing setup.

**How it earns.** ₹10,000 setup per salon (~₹7,500 kept) plus ₹699 a month (₹8,388 a year; ~₹6,384 kept).

**Can it reach ₹1 crore a year?** The arithmetic, which all three agree on:
- Monthly fees alone need about **1,192 paying salons**. Counting setups as well (at 3% of salons leaving each month), about **834**.
- Today's plan, 3 new salons a month with 3% leaving, **levels off near 100 salons**: about ₹8.4 lakh a year in fees, or ~₹12 lakh with setups. A good small business, not a crore.
- Holding 1,192 salons needs ~36 new salons **every month**, twelve times the year-1 plan.
- All 86 qualifying Bhubaneswar salons would bring ~₹7.2 lakh a year in fees, so ₹1 crore means roughly **10 cities** like it, won and kept.
- At ₹1,999 a month (a rival's mid plan) it would need ~417 salons.
- $1 million is larger still; the board left the exchange rate and the number of qualifying salons in other cities as open questions.

**What would have to be true:** a way to win and set up salons without the founder (product distributors, trained local setters); churn at or below 3%; a higher price once there is proof; and enough 5+ chair salons in the next cities (open question → `/founder-competitors` or a Google Maps count per city).

## Where they disagree
Product wants features cut so the pitch is one sentence ("Which three would you keep?"). Offers wants the full stack kept but sold on proof, at a higher price. Monopoly cares less about features and most about distribution and an advantage that compounds.

## Conditions (checklist)
- [ ] Build live sync; run it in a real 5+ chair salon for 30 days with every stylist on their own phone, and show the live view matching the chairs (all three)
- [ ] Sign the first 5 salons; each pays the first ₹5,000; stylists bill on 20+ days (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] Record real numbers: demo-to-yes rate, visits per setup, cost to win a salon, rupees of leakage found, 30-day usage, 3-month churn (all three)
- [ ] Time real stylists billing at rush hour; publish the number; show what the owner sees when an entry is missed (Product)
- [ ] Say what it does in one sentence; hide features that don't serve it in version one (Product)
- [ ] Turn the first 5 into proof (leakage found, a short owner video, owners who take calls), then raise the price from salon 6 onward (Offers)
- [ ] Write down the setup and support routine so someone else can do it; test one channel that doesn't need the founder, such as a product distributor, and price it (Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] Name one advantage that grows with every salon, and start it (Monopoly)
- [ ] Count qualifying salons in the next 3 cities before expanding (Offers, Monopoly)

## The strongest version the board can see
Not quite the app as pitched. The strongest version is **"we bring your lost customers back, and we set it up and stay with you"**: the reminder, referral and wallet loop, proven with real rupees in 5 Bhubaneswar salons, with the live view as the demo moment rather than the promise. It is priced higher than today once that proof exists, sold through people who already visit salons (product distributors, local setters) as well as the founder, and grown city by city. At ₹699 and one founder it is a solid local business that levels off near 100 salons. Reaching ₹1 crore needs the price, the sales channel and the support team to change, not just more effort.
