# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The market is narrower than in round 1. Salons with 5 or more chairs, good reviews and strong local search rank are a small, defined group. That is the right shape for a market you can win first and grow from.
- The buyer has something to protect: their standing, repeat customers and staff discipline. Owners who are often away from the salon need to see the floor from anywhere, and the "live salon" view targets that directly.
- The cash-leakage checks are real, specific and already built. They include bill paid once, cash counted against expected cash, unusual-bill flags and owner approval for services not on the list. That is know-how about how salons lose money, not just a feature list.
- Data could build some lock-in. Once a salon's customers, wallets, referrals and staff history are inside the app, switching costs something.
- Running costs are low against the price: about ₹35-45 a month with one-tap WhatsApp, or about ₹640 with automatic WhatsApp. The ₹10,000 upfront fee pays for setup and training.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No way to reach buyers.** The sales channel is not decided. Selling one salon at a time, in person, across tier 1, 2 and 3 cities is slow and expensive. The cost to win one salon is unknown. A good product without distribution is not a business.
2. **The edge is unproven and easy to copy.** "No one offers a live floor" has not been checked against Zylu, MioSalon or others. Even if it is true, a live floor view is a small step forward, not something ten times better. An established competitor with a sales team could add it within months. Nothing here is a network effect, scale advantage or brand yet.
3. **The core claim is not built.** Live sync is planned but not built, and today each phone keeps its own data. The "live salon" differentiator does not exist yet. The round 1 objection, that staff do the data entry while the owner gets the benefit, also still stands. If barbers do not enter walk-ins at rush hour, the live floor shows nothing.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do Zylu, MioSalon and the others charge, and does any of them already show a live floor?
- Which one city, or one part of a city, will you dominate first? How many 5+ chair salons with good reviews are there?
- How do the first 100 salons arrive, and what does each one cost to win and set up?
- What do you know about salon owners that Zylu and MioSalon do not?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the right moment?
- What stops a funded competitor from copying the live floor in month six?
- Who provides Sunday support, and how many salons can one founder serve before it breaks?
- Can anything here grow stronger with more users, such as referrals between salons, benchmarks across salons or a shared customer wallet? Or does every salon stay an island?
- What are your budget, time and churn assumptions?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Research the competitors with sources and links. Confirm whether anyone offers a live floor and at what price. If someone does, the case for this business has to be restated.
2. Pick one city as the starting market. Count the 5+ chair salons with good reviews there, and aim for a large share of that count before expanding.
3. Test one sales channel with real salons and report the real cost to win and set up one salon, set against ₹10,000 upfront plus ₹500 a month or less.
4. Build live sync and run it in at least a few paying salons. Show that staff enter walk-ins at rush hour, so the live floor is real.
5. Name the long-term advantage: what this business could build that a copycat cannot easily match, such as data lock-in, a network across salons, or a brand among top-rated salons.
