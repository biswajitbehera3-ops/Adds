# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The new target is a better market than round 1. Salons with 5 or more chairs, good reviews and strong local search have money, have more to lose from leakage, and are easy to find: they are the ones that show up in local search.
- The pain is real and costly. Cash leakage, staff discipline and repeat customers touch the owner's money every day.
- The dream outcome is clear: know your money is safe and see your floor from anywhere. "Live salon" is easy to picture and easy to sell in one sentence.
- The stack is already wide: billing, wallet, WhatsApp bills and reminders, referrals, attendance, and a long list of misuse checks. Several of these (cash count against expected cash, unusual-bill flag, owner approval for off-list services) speak straight to the owner's fear.
- Setup is done for the owner (prices, staff and logo entered, team trained). That cuts the time and effort the owner has to give.
- The upfront fee now sits with a buyer who can afford it, and the founder did not just cut price to win.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The monthly price may not cover the monthly cost.** The brief says ₹500 a month or less. Running cost with automatic WhatsApp is about ₹640 a month per salon. Every salon on automatic WhatsApp would lose money each month before fixed costs (about ₹600 a month). The ₹10,000 upfront fee would slowly be eaten by the subscription. Only the one-tap WhatsApp option (about ₹35-45) leaves a margin, and that asks more effort of the salon.
2. **Likelihood is close to zero today.** The buyer has no reason to believe it works. The headline feature, live sync, is not built, so "live salon" cannot be shown working across phones. No real salon uses it. No owner they know uses it (a round 1 objection). There is no guarantee. A ₹10,000 upfront fee with no proof and no guarantee is the hardest offer to say yes to.
3. **The effort lands on the staff, the benefit on the owner.** The owner pays, but barbers must enter every walk-in, at rush hour, on their own phones. Many of the checks exist to catch those same staff. If staff do not enter data, the owner sees an empty floor and the whole value falls apart. The 1-minute entry is a target, not a measured result.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Is the monthly fee meant to include automatic WhatsApp? If yes, how does ₹500 or less cover about ₹640 in cost?
- What will the guarantee be on the ₹10,000? What happens if the staff stop using it after a month?
- How many 5+ chair salons with good reviews are there in the cities you will start in? (Not known yet.)
- What does it cost to win one salon, with in-person sales and setup across tier 1-3 cities? How many salons does one ₹10,000 fee pay to win?
- Can you show a real barber entering a real customer in 1 minute, during a rush?
- How much cash leakage does a typical target salon have? Can you show one owner the money the app found?
- What do Zylu and MioSalon charge, and does either already show a live floor? If one does, what is left that makes this the obvious choice?
- Could you charge more per month and still be the obvious choice? What would you have to add or prove?
- Who answers the phone on Sunday?
- Has any real owner been asked to pay the new price? The panel was simulated, at the old price, and was not re-run.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Fix the monthly price against the cost.** Either price the plan with automatic WhatsApp above its running cost, or make one-tap WhatsApp the default and sell automatic messages as a paid add-on. No plan should lose money per salon each month.
2. **Build live sync and prove it in real salons first.** Put it in a small number of real 5+ chair salons. Measure entry time at rush hour, how many walk-ins staff actually enter, and leakage found. Use those numbers and those owners as the proof in every pitch.
3. **Add a guarantee tied to the upfront fee**, one the founder can afford, so the owner is not betting ₹10,000 on trust alone (round 1's "upfront fee before seeing results" objection).
4. **Give staff a reason to enter data.** Add something in the stack that helps the barber, not just the owner, so the 1-minute entry actually happens.
5. **Check Zylu and MioSalon before claiming "no one offers this".** If the live floor is not unique, find the edge that is, other than price.
6. **Pre-sell at the real price.** Get real owners to pay (or commit) at ₹10,000 upfront before more building, and record the cost to win each one.
