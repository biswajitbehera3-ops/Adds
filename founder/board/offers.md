# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is a real money pain inside the offer: cash leaking out of the drawer, cancelled bills and staff taking walk-ins off the books. The misuse checks (owner cash count against expected cash, "left without service" log, unusual-bill flag, WhatsApp codes on wallet spends) attack it directly. That is a dream outcome an owner can put a rupee figure on, not a "nice to have".
- One clear segment responds: owners who are often away (33% buy in the panel), 5-8 chair salons (20%) and premium/franchise (20%). These owners have the pain and the money.
- Several stack items are cheap to deliver and valuable to the buyer: WhatsApp due-back reminders, the prepaid wallet with top-up bonus (brings cash in early), the ₹100 referral. These can make the app pay for itself in money the owner can see, not just in tidier records.
- Done-for-you setup (prices, staff and logo entered, team trained at the salon) lowers effort, which is where most offers lose.
- 83 automated tests and the misuse checks are already built, so the stack is not imaginary.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The headline promise is not built, so likelihood is near zero.** "See from anywhere" and the owner's daily cash and misuse checks depend on sync between phones, which does not exist yet; each phone keeps its own data. The segment most likely to buy (the owner who is away) is buying exactly the feature that does not work. Add no proof from any real salon, "no owner I know uses it" and fear of data loss, and the buyer has no reason to believe it will work. An offer that cannot deliver its lead outcome should not be sold.
2. **The market is mostly the wrong market.** 75 of 100 simulated buyers run 1-4 chairs, where buying was 0-10%, and that panel is likely to overstate buying. These owners count every rupee, trust a register and are hard to reach one by one. How salons will find the product is not decided, and the cost to win one salon is unknown. A good offer to a market with little money and no cheap way to reach it still fails.
3. **The effort and the price are both pointed the wrong way.** The offer asks staff to take a selfie and type every walk-in's mobile number at rush hour, which buyers called both slow and distrustful. Then it charges ₹4,999 before the owner has seen any result, and ₹999 a month sits at the top of the panel's acceptable range (about ₹206-1,003, median "too expensive" ₹1,200). Price was 24 of the pass reasons and "no setup fee until it works" was the most repeated flip (~65). There is no guarantee to carry the risk for the buyer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- When will sync between the owner's and stylists' phones work, with an online backup, and what does it cost to run per salon per month (server plus WhatsApp messages)?
- How much cash does a typical 5-8 chair salon lose a month to leakage today, and can you show that number in a real salon before and after?
- Which salon, by name, will be your first proof, and will its owner talk to the owner next door?
- How will the first 20 salons find you, and what does winning one cost in time and money?
- What does the walk-in entry look like at rush hour, in seconds per customer, and can staff skip the selfie?
- How do you compare with Zylu and MioSalon on price and features? (Not researched yet.)
- What churn do you expect after the free month and in slow months?
- What would you guarantee, and could you afford it?
- What is your budget and how many hours a week can you give this?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Build sync and an online backup before selling anything that says "from anywhere". Until then, do not put that line in the pitch.
2. Narrow the first market to owners who are often away and 5-8 chair or multi-branch salons. Leave 1-2 chair barbers out for now.
3. Move the setup fee behind a result: charge it only after the free month, and only if staff are still using the app (the panel's top flip). Back it with a guarantee tied to the outcome the owner cares about, for example the cash count matching or the month free, sized once running costs are known.
4. Get proof: 3-5 real salons running it for a month, with the leakage found and the repeat visits from reminders recorded in rupees, and owners willing to be called by other owners.
5. Cut staff effort at rush hour (fast walk-in entry, mobile number optional, selfie check-in optional or replaced) and add Sunday support to the stack.
6. Answer the open numbers (cost to win a salon, running cost per salon, churn, budget) before setting the final price. Test whether a lower small-shop plan (the panel named ₹299-399, customer features only) is worth offering, rather than cutting the main price.
