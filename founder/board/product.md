# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The new target is sharper. One kind of buyer: a 5+ chair salon that is already doing well and wants to keep it that way.
- The pitch now starts from a moment, not a feature list. The owner is away and opens the phone to see who is in which chair right now. That is a clear, visual experience to build back from.
- The data entry goal moved the right way. One minute per walk-in, done by the barber, is a real experience target, and it answers the top objection from round 1 (staff will not enter data at rush hour).
- Craft shows in the parts nobody sees. 83 automated tests and a long list of cash-leakage checks (bill paid once, cash count against expected, undo kept with a reason) are care where a buyer will never look.
- The salon's own name, logo and prices inside the app make it feel like their tool, not a generic one.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The one thing does not exist yet.** The "live salon" view depends on live sync, and live sync is not built. Today each phone keeps its own data. The headline experience is a drawing until sync works, and sync is where shared, multi-phone apps most often break (lost bills, double entries, two phones disagreeing). One wrong number on the owner's floor view and trust is gone.
2. **Too many things for one founder.** Billing, wallet, WhatsApp bills and reminders, referrals, attendance, leave, calendar, misuse checks, live floor, backups, and a liquid-glass redesign still to build. That is many seams and a lot to keep working. The product does not yet say no to anything.
3. **The live floor is only as true as the barber's entry.** The owner gets the benefit; the staff do the work. If a barber skips or delays an entry during a rush, the live floor is wrong, and the owner sees it as the app being wrong. The handoff between the barber's phone and the owner's view is the seam most likely to break the experience. The one-minute entry is a target, not yet a measured result.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what does this app do? Is it "see your salon live from anywhere", or "stop cash leakage", or "fast billing"? Pick one.
- If you had to ship with only three features, which three? What would you cut first?
- Walk me through the first five minutes after setup for the owner, and for a barber with a customer waiting. What do they each tap?
- Have you timed a real barber entering a real walk-in? Is it under one minute at rush hour, in their language?
- What does the owner see on the live floor when a barber forgets to enter a customer? How does the app catch it?
- What happens when two phones edit the same bill, or one phone is offline for an hour? Has sync been tested on real salon networks?
- Where else does the experience pass between people or systems (WhatsApp delivery, payment collection, the salon code, setup), and what does the customer see when each one fails?
- Who answers the phone on a Sunday when it breaks? That is part of the product too.
- Why build the liquid-glass design now, before sync works?
- The app is English only today. Is that the experience a barber in a tier-3 city needs?
- Have you checked whether Zylu, MioSalon or anyone else already shows a live floor? The claim is untested.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Build and prove live sync first, before any new feature or the redesign. Run it in at least one real 5+ chair salon with every barber on their own phone, and show the owner's floor matching what is really in the chairs.
2. Name the one thing in one sentence, and cut or hide every feature that does not serve it for the first version.
3. Time real barbers entering real walk-ins at rush hour and show the one-minute target is met. Show how the app handles a missed entry.
4. Map every handoff (barber to owner, app to WhatsApp, app to payment, setup to first day) and show what the user sees when each one fails.
5. Check the "no one offers a live floor" claim against Zylu, MioSalon and others before building the pitch on it.
