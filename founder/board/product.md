# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The idea starts from a real moment: the owner who is away and wants to know what is happening in the salon and what cash should be in the drawer. That is a clear experience to build around, and the buyers most likely to say yes are the ones who feel it (owner often away: 33% buy, against 9% overall).
- The parts nobody sees show care. Bills are paid once, cancellations and staff undos are kept with a reason, there is a clock-set-back check, staff see only the last 4 digits of customer numbers, and there are 83 automated tests. That is craft in places the customer will never look.
- Each salon gets an app with its own name and logo, set up and taught in person. That puts the brand and the first few hours of use in the founder's hands rather than leaving them to a download page.
- The pitch already talks about what the owner sees and what the customer gets, not the technology.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The main promise is not built.** "See from anywhere" needs online sync between phones, and there is none: each phone keeps its own data. The owner view is the one thing worth paying for, and without sync it is a demo. The same gap creates the "data loss if a phone breaks or the company shuts" objection. Until the phones talk to each other, the product is a set of separate apps.
2. **The work lands on the people who gain least from it.** The owner gets the benefit, but the stylist pays for it at the busiest moment: a selfie check-in, typing every walk-in's number at rush hour, billing in the app. Buyers say staff will not do it, that selfies make staff feel distrusted, and that some customers will not give a number. If stylists skip it on a busy Sunday, the owner's cash check and dashboard show wrong numbers, and the product loses trust in the one place it has to be trusted.
3. **It does too many things.** Billing, walk-ins, prepaid wallet, WhatsApp bills and reminders, referrals, attendance and leave, a live floor, top customers and a list of misuse checks: that is about ten products in one pitch, and the pitch takes a paragraph. Every feature adds a handoff between people, phones and WhatsApp, and that is where things go wrong. The 0% buy rate among 1-2 chair barbers, and about 28 buyers asking for a small plan with only the customer features, suggest different buyers want different single things.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing this does for the owner? If the answer is "know today's real takings and cash from anywhere", what can you cut that does not serve it?
- What do the first five minutes look like for a stylist at 6 pm on a Saturday with three walk-ins waiting? How many taps and how many seconds does one bill take? Have you timed it in a real salon?
- When will sync work, and what happens to the owner view when a stylist's phone is offline, broken or reset?
- What happens when a stylist does not enter a walk-in? How does the owner find out the numbers are wrong, and how does the product keep from blaming the wrong person?
- Who answers the phone on a busy Sunday, and who sends the WhatsApp messages until server-sent messages exist?
- Would the product be better with no selfie check-in? What does the owner actually lose without it?
- Is Hindi (or the local language) part of the core experience for stylists, rather than an add-on?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Build working online sync with a cloud backup before selling to any salon on the "see from anywhere" promise, and show the owner view staying correct while stylists' phones go offline and come back.
2. Choose the one thing and cut the pitch to one sentence; move everything that does not serve it out of the first version (for example referrals, wallet bonuses or selfie check-in).
3. Time a walk-in bill in a real salon at rush hour and bring it down to something a stylist will actually do every time; show staff still using it after the free month in at least one real salon.
4. Decide who owns the experience after setup (Sunday support, WhatsApp sending, data recovery) and run it end to end, so there is no point where a problem is no one's job.
