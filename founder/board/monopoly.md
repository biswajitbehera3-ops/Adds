# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- A possible small market to own first: 5-8 chair salons whose owner is often away. They are the only groups where the simulated panel bought at a real rate (20% of 5-8 chair salons, 33% of owner-often-away), and the problem they have (staff misuse, cash leaking from the drawer) is specific.
- The misuse checks are the most distinctive part of the product: owner cash count against expected cash, "left without service" and undo logged with reasons, WhatsApp codes for referrals and large wallet spends, clock-set-back check. This is the only place where it could be clearly better than a generic billing app rather than slightly better.
- The customer side (wallet, referrals, WhatsApp reminders) could, once many salons in one area use it, become something salons and customers both depend on. Today it is not; each salon's data stands alone.
- Cheap to build: one founder with AI help has a working prototype with 83 tests passing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A crowded market with no clear edge.** Buyers already named Zylu and MioSalon, and neither has been researched. Billing, wallet, reminders, staff attendance and WhatsApp bills look like the standard feature list for salon software. Nothing here is roughly ten times better on something that matters; at best it is an incremental improvement, and per-salon branding is easy to copy. Competing on this list means competing on price, and the panel already pushes price down (least resistance about ₹306 a month against ₹999 asked).
2. **No distribution.** How salons will find it is "not decided yet," and it is sold and set up in person, salon by salon. The panel said proof from a nearby salon or a distributor would flip about 30 no's, and 0% of owners who ask other owners first would buy. There is no answer yet to how the first 100 salons arrive or what each costs, and in-person setup and training sets a floor on that cost.
3. **The core promise is not built, so there is nothing durable yet.** "See your salon from anywhere" depends on online sync between phones, which does not exist. Without it the owner-away segment, the one with real pull, has no reason to buy. Running sync and server-sent WhatsApp also adds per-salon costs nobody has measured, and nothing in the plan (no network effect, no scale advantage, no brand) holds a position for ten years.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do Zylu and MioSalon do and charge, and what, specifically, does this do ten times better than them?
- What is the secret: what do you know about salon owners, staff misuse or Indian salon cash handling that the existing players have missed?
- Which exact small market do you take first (one city, one segment) and what share of it could you hold within a year? How many 5-8 chair salons are in it?
- How do the first 100 salons arrive, and what does winning one cost, including the in-person setup and training?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this possible or needed today?
- What stops a competitor copying the misuse checks in month six?
- When is online sync built, and what does it cost per salon per month to run with WhatsApp messages?
- Is there a path to a network effect, for example a wallet or referral that works across salons, or a distributor relationship that others cannot get?
- How much time and money can you put in, and who else is on the team for sales and Sunday support?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). The vote would move to FUND IF with: a researched comparison showing a clear, large edge over Zylu and MioSalon on owner-away misuse control; online sync working; one named city and segment as the first market; and a tested channel (such as a distributor or a referral from a nearby salon) with a measured cost to win one salon.
