# Finding streamers who pay clippers, how they pay, and YouTube's own pay — 8 Nov 2026

## 1. Where streamers recruit clippers

| Where | How to find it | Competition |
|---|---|---|
| **Content Rewards / Whop** | Discover page → Gaming category; search the streamer's name | High (public) |
| **Vyro** | Campaigns page; turn on notifications | High; region set per campaign |
| **Streamer Discord servers** | Join the streamer's Discord and look for a `#clipping`, `#clippers` or `#content` channel with the rules, rate and submission link | **Lower**, because these are harder to find |
| **X (Twitter)** | Search `"clippers wanted"`, `"clipping program"`, `"<streamer> clipping"` | Medium |
| **Twitch / Kick panels** | Read the panels under the stream; programs are often linked there | Lower |
| **Kick's own program** | Kick itself has paid clippers about $40–50 per 100K views (reported April 2026), through streamer Discords and its program | Medium |
| **Direct pitch** | Mid-size streamers (10K–200K followers) with nobody clipping them | **Lowest**. You set the deal. |

Scale reference: one streamer (N3on) reportedly works with about 1,000 paid clippers. MrBeast pays his own clippers about $50 per 100K views.

## 2. How you get paid

| Type | How pay is calculated | When and how you're paid |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace** (Content Rewards / Whop, Vyro) | Per 1,000 verified views, up to a per-post cap, from the campaign budget | Into your platform wallet. Content Rewards settles about 10 days after approval; Vyro pays weekly (min $10) through Stripe or PayPal. Some clippers report about a 10% platform fee. |
| **Streamer Discord program** | Usually $0.50–$2 per 1,000 views, often **only after a minimum** (for example 100K views, counted across your clips) | Paid directly by the streamer's team, usually PayPal or crypto. **No platform protection.** |
| **Direct deal** | Flat fee per clip, or a monthly retainer | You invoice. Ask for 50% upfront, and use PayPal invoices, Wise or Payoneer. |

**Safety:** never pay to join a clipping program. Off-platform deals have no escrow, so get paid weekly instead of monthly until you
trust them. Foreign income from India is taxable, so keep records of every payment.

## 3. Can you also earn from YouTube itself? Yes, but much later and far less

YouTube's ad money comes from the **YouTube Partner Program (YPP)**, which is separate from campaign money. You can earn both on the same
Short.

**Requirements (Nov 2026):**
- **Ads tier:** 1,000 subscribers **plus** either **10 million Shorts views in 90 days** or 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months.
  Shorts views and watch hours don't combine.
- **Fan Funding tier** (no ad money, only memberships and Super Thanks): 500 subscribers plus 3 million Shorts views in 90 days.
- **From 1 Feb 2027:** new applicants need **20 million Shorts views in 90 days** or 8,000 watch hours. To keep earning on Shorts, a
  channel must maintain 10 million qualified Shorts views every 90 days.

**How much Shorts pay:** creators get 45% of a shared pool, which works out to roughly **$0.01–$0.35 per 1,000 views**. That's
$10–$350 per million views, and audiences in India sit at the low end.

**Compare:** a campaign at $1–$1.50 per 1,000 views pays **$1,000–$1,500 per million views**, which is 3 to 100 times more than
YouTube's own Shorts pay.

**Risks for clip channels:**
- **Reused content:** YPP rejects channels that just repost others' footage. Our edits (captions, hooks, graphics, zoom-punch,
  commentary) make clips transformative, but approval isn't guaranteed.
- **Content ID claims:** if a streamer or Rockstar claims a Short, its ad money goes to them.

**Bottom line:** for the first months, **campaign money is the real income**. A new channel is unlikely to reach 10M Shorts views in
90 days before the February 2027 change, so treat YPP as a later bonus once the page is big.
