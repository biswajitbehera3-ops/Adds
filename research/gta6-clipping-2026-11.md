## Rockstar's rules (official policy, last updated 10 Jan 2025)
Rockstar is "happy for fans to upload footage of their gameplay". These get **taken down**:
- **Pre-release or leaked footage**, including "early unboxing" videos. Nothing before 19 Nov.
- **Spoilers:** the ending, or "an edited combination of cutscenes and/or in-game plot dialogue". **Isolated cinematics are removed.**
- **In-game TV and comedy shows** posted on their own.
- **Cheats, glitches and money exploits, mod menus, account selling, unauthorized mods or ports.**

The written policy covers "occasional, non-commercial" fan use, and defines non-commercial as not making money from the footage.
In practice, monetized gameplay channels and streamer clipping campaigns are widespread, but **paid clipping of GTA footage is outside
what the policy explicitly allows**. Stay strictly away from the takedown list, and prefer campaigns where a streamer or brand funds
the clipping, because the brief covers their footage.

## YouTube rules that affect GTA clips
- **Age restriction (since 17 Nov 2025):** realistic torture or mass violence against non-combatants can be age-restricted, which kills
  reach and may not count as verified views. **Never post a rampage against civilians.** Blur the most graphic moment.
- **Ads (Sept 2026):** violent gameplay in the **first 7 seconds** blocks ads. Keep openings clean.

## Which GTA 6 clips earn
- **Clips where something resolves:** the plan fails, the stunt lands, the streamer is proven wrong. Loud clips with no payoff die.
- **Driving, stunts, game systems, comedy, glitches and funny physics** beat rampages.
- **Clip the player, not the plot:** reactions, chases and failures, never story reveals.
- **First 1.5 seconds** open on the payoff. Burned-in captions, because most people watch muted. Post consistently in one niche.
