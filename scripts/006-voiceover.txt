10 HIDDEN DETAILS IN GTA 6 THAT WILL GET YOU CAUGHT — VOICEOVER (plain text, edit freely)

[HOOK]
Before you rob your first store in GTA 6, you need to know which ten hidden details get you caught, and how one Rockstar developer got around the biggest one.

Most of these come from Edge magazine's ninety-minute look at the game, and GameSpot's breakdown of the Extended Look. Every one has its source on screen. And the cops in this game don't just chase you. They build a case.

[#10 — THEY CLOCK YOUR GUN]
In GTA 5, you could pull a shotgun out on a busy street and nobody blinked. Rob Nelson from Rockstar says that doesn't work anymore. Now people notice. And they can tell the difference between a gun on your back and a gun in your hands. Some walk away, some run, and some will come and challenge you. The good news: carrying one doesn't automatically mean a wanted level. Put it away in time, and it calms down.

[#9 — THE VICTIM FIGHTS BACK]
When you rob someone, don't turn your back. Edge says a person you're robbing might hand it over, then go for you the second you turn around. And in a store, another customer can decide to be a hero. Robberies don't follow a script anymore.

[#8 — THE OWNER CALLS IT IN]
You stole a car, nobody chased you, you think you're clean. You're not. Edge says even if the car has no tracker, the owner can still report it stolen. So a car you took ten minutes ago can come back to bite you, long after you drove away.

[#7 — THE CAR SNITCHES]
And if the car's expensive, it's worse. Expensive modern cars can have trackers that keep broadcasting where they are after you steal them. If you're stealing to sell to a fence, there's good news: they have signal blockers at the drop-off. And there's a catch: they pay you less if you wreck it. So you're running from the cops while trying not to scratch the paint.

[#6 — THE SEARCHLIGHT]
At night, hiding in the dark isn't enough. Police helicopters use searchlights to find you. Rockstar's whole idea is that you escape smart, through back alleys and abandoned cars, instead of just flooring it until the stars go away.

[#5 — THEY TALK TO EACH OTHER]
From here, it gets worse, because now the cops start working together. In Edge's demo, the police talk over the radio and lay a stinger strip across the road. It was night, Rob Nelson didn't see it, the front tyre burst, and Jason and Lucia went over the handlebars. They tried to run, and a police dog caught Jason and pinned him to the ground. One radio call, and the escape was over.

[#4 — THE STARS CHANGE COLOUR]
Six stars are back. But watch their colour, because GameSpot's breakdown says it tells you what the cops know. Solid white: they can see you. Flashing: you've broken line of sight. Solid red: you're still near the scene, and they're looking, but they don't know exactly where you are.

[#3 — THE POLICE REPORT]
And under those stars is the most important thing in this video. Those little icons under the stars are the police report. Edge says they show exactly what the cops know about you. A camera means you're on CCTV, so you'll need to destroy the footage. A hanger means they know your clothes, so change, but not where anyone can see you. Two people means they saw Jason and Lucia together, so split up. A gun means they know your weapon, so ditch it. A car means they know your car. And a face means they know your face, and GameSpot thinks a mask is the answer to that one.

[#2 — HOLLOW STARS]
Now, here's what happens when you get all of that right. Hollow stars. GameSpot says that means the police have no idea who they're looking for. Watch the Extended Look right after the jewellery-store job: the getaway rider pulls away with two hollow stars, and look under them. The icons are gone. No face, no clothes, no car. They're still wanted, and the cops have nothing.

[#1 — NO WITNESSES, NO REPORT]
And number one starts before you commit the crime. In GTA 6, a crime only gets reported if someone sees it or an alarm goes off. And people remember what they've seen. So Phil Hooker from Rockstar North found a way to empty a store before robbing it. In his words: "I basically started picking fights with them, provoking them and then immediately defusing it. Then they would leave the store, so that would clear them out and I could rob it more cleanly." No punches thrown. No witnesses. No report.
